To measure Kazakhstan's transformation, we created original graphs using data from the Central State Archive and Kozybayev research. Graph 1 shows Kazakhstan's share in Soviet wartime production: 85% of lead, 35% of copper, 60% of molybdenum, and 65% of bismuth. Nine out of ten Soviet bullets were made from Kazakh lead, while Karaganda coal output grew by 80% to replace occupied Donbas. Graph 2 shows 220 factories and over 20 cultural institutions evacuated to Kazakhstan. Alma-Ata became the wartime cinema capital. Mosfilm and Lenfilm merged there into the Central United Film Studio, producing 80% of Soviet wartime films, including Eisenstein Ivan the Terrible. Scientific evacuation also led to the founding out of the Kazakh Academy of Sciences in 1946. In conclusion, World War II turned Kazakhs from agrarian region into an industrial and scientific center, but at the cost over 600,000 lives. The front and the rear worked as one. Kazakh soldiers fought with the weapons made from Kazakh metal.
