To understand Kazakh wartime experience, we apply a two-level comparative approach: first comparing two legendary military formations formed in Kazakhstan, the second comparing military mobilization with labor mobilization. On the frontline over 1.2 billion residents of Kazakhstan were drafted into the Red Army in addition to 700,000 people mobilized into the labor army. Let us compare two famous divisions formed on Kazakh soil: the 316th Rifle Division, later the 8th Guard Panfilov Division formed in Alma-Ata, and the 310th Rifle Division formed in Akmolinsk. While the 316th Division defended the Volokolamsk highway during the Battle of Moscow in November 1941 using anti-tank defense tactics developed by Bauyrzhan Momyshuly, the 310th Division fought in the swamp and forest near Leningrad to break the blockade. Despite completely different geography and combat tactics, both divisions shared multi-ethnic composition, high casualty rates, and became symbols of the frontline resilience. When we compare the frontlines with the home front, we see two sides of total war. While the frontlines suffered over 600,000 war dead, nearly half of all those conscripted, the home front experienced a radical demographic and economic shift. Over 530,000 evacuees and more than one million deported people arrived in Kazakhstan, while women, teenagers, and evacuees replaced mobilized men in factories and collective farms. Behind me in this museum exhibition you can see all the personal belongings, combat medals, and archival photographs of the Kazakh soldiers of the Panfilov Guard and the Kazakh Heroes of the Soviet Union. About 500 soldiers from Kazakhstan received the highest title, proving that units from the steppe played a critical role in the main strategic battles of the war.
